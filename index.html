Human-Centered AI for the Physical World

I'm a senior undergraduate in Computer Science at Purdue University. I work with Prof. Karthik Ramani ↗ in the C Design Lab on human–AI interaction and AR agents, and with Dr. Jingkang Yang ↗ on long-horizon egocentric memory and scalable data infrastructure for embodied agents. Previously, I worked with Prof. Yung-Hsiang Lu on generative modeling and edge AI, and spent two summers at Eyedaptic ↗ building AI systems for AR glasses.

My current interests are human-centered agents for the physical world: how AI can understand a 3D environment well enough to reason about and carry out tasks in it, and how XR and wearable interfaces can enable low-friction interaction with capable agents that continuously share the user’s point of view. I’m also studying how long-horizon first-person experience can serve two roles at once: persistent memory for agents and structured, action-centric training data for embodied learning.

Human–AI · XR2026 — PresentSubmitted · ACM CHI 2027

Situated human–AI reasoning in AR

How can an AR agent reason over the same physical task as the wearer without hiding the state it is using?

I built a stateful, tool-using reasoning agent that combines first-person scene context with persistent spatial state. It can choose when to observe, query spatial information, or ask the wearer, while separating generative reasoning from deterministic execution so corrections revise only the reasoning branches that depend on them.

My role: research framing, agent architecture, state/memory design, first-person 2D/3D scene understanding, human-in-the-loop interaction, Unity/Quest implementation, and study instrumentation.

Purdue C Design Lab ↗ · advised by Prof. Karthik Ramani ↗
XR Authoring · Agents2025 — 2026Submitted · ACM CHI 2027

In-situ authoring for embodied agents in AR

How can people author spatial agent behavior without translating what they mean in the room into low-level scripts?

I introduced an experience–episode–unit interaction graph for representing spatial context, agent behavior, and conditional transitions, paired with a dual-view AR workflow for in-situ authoring and world-in-miniature overview. A multimodal compiler/runtime turns speech, gaze, pointing, and pose into executable agent behavior.

My role: interaction representation, authoring workflow, multimodal compiler/runtime, Unity/Meta Quest implementation, and evaluation.

Purdue C Design Lab ↗ · advised by Prof. Karthik Ramani ↗
Egocentric AI · Memory & Data Infrastructure2026 — Present

Long-horizon memory and egocentric data infrastructure

How can hours of first-person experience become both persistent memory and reusable supervision for embodied agents?

I work on event-centric memory, hierarchical retrieval, and tool-based inspection for recovering the right event, time, object state, or interaction from long egocentric recordings without repeatedly reprocessing the full stream.

In parallel, I build scalable pipelines that transform raw first-person video into structured, action-centric training data—linking observations, interactions, state changes, and temporal context so egocentric experience can be reused for embodied learning.

My role: long-video reasoning and memory, retrieval design, egocentric data processing infrastructure, post-training support, benchmark design, and systems-level accuracy–latency analysis.

Egocentric AI research · supervised by Dr. Jingkang Yang ↗
3D Generation · Evaluation2025 — May 2026

Accuracy-oriented 3D piano hand motion generation

A hand pose can look plausible and still press the wrong key.

I reframed MIDI-conditioned hand-motion generation around time-indexed 3D key-contact targets and developed contact-aware flow-matching objectives. I also built a hard-passage evaluation for large jumps, dense chords, and fast transitions, where wrong fingers and timing errors are much easier to expose.

My role: project lead; problem formulation, target representation, learning objectives, event-local refinement, and task-specific evaluation.

Purdue ECE ↗ · advised by Prof. Yung-Hsiang Lu ↗
Generative AI · Edge AI2025 — May 2026

Compositional and hardware-aware evaluation of text-to-image models

How much of a model’s apparent image quality survives when we test compositional correctness and real edge-inference cost?

I benchmarked text-to-image generators across object, count, attribute, spatial-relation, and style factors, adapted CLIP into a task-specific semantic-alignment evaluator, and built a reproducible Jetson Orin inference/profiling stack with standardized timing and GPU-memory measurement.

My role: benchmark design, prompt taxonomy, CLIP adaptation, automated evaluation, edge deployment, and inference profiling.

Purdue ECE ↗ · advised by Prof. Yung-Hsiang Lu ↗
Wearable AI · Voice AgentsSummer 2026

Voice-first video assistance for AR glasses

What changes when a voice assistant on glasses can keep state, use tools, and remember what the user tends to choose?

At Eyedaptic ↗, I architected a stateful, tool-using voice agent for AR glasses, using typed tools for search, refinement, selection, history resume, and playback. I also built a retrieval and personalization layer with multi-signal reranking, channel-aware context, and local behavioral memory.

My role: agent/tool architecture, retrieval and ranking, personalization memory, Android/backend integration, and evaluation.

Where I\'m pushing next. I\'m especially interested in agents that can build a useful understanding of a 3D environment and carry out tasks inside it—a capability that also matters for embodied intelligence—and in smart-glasses agents that can turn persistent first-person context into useful action with very little user input.

About

More than research & coursework.

I'm probably more interested in products than in any one technology. What interests me most is the psychology underneath them: people differ, but patterns in attention, expectation, habit, trust, and hesitation repeat more often than we think.

I usually start by building a rough model of the person before the interface—what they are likely to notice, assume, ignore, or do next—and then ask what the product should make easier or disappear entirely.

That is part of why smart glasses interest me. If agents become good enough at understanding first-person context and using tools, a very small amount of input could unlock useful experiences without repeatedly pulling someone back to a screen.

How I think

First principles. I like reducing a messy problem until I can see the few relationships that actually matter. Daoist philosophy probably has something to do with that.

People before products. I like looking for recurring human patterns—attention, expectation, habit, trust, hesitation—and asking what a product should do with them.

AI-native building. I learned software engineering while coding agents were becoming useful, so tools like Codex and Claude feel normal to me—just another part of how I build.
