Human–AI · XR2026 — PresentUnder review

Situated human–AI reasoning in AR

An AR assistant can give plausible guidance while the user has no idea what it actually saw or what part of the scene its answer depends on.

I built a Quest-based prototype that keeps task-relevant observations, interpretations, and user corrections as explicit task state instead of burying everything in chat history. Visual claims are tied to detector-backed references and Quest geometry, and a correction only revises the parts of the task state that depend on it.

My role: research framing, state representation, system architecture, perception and grounding, interaction design, Unity/Quest implementation, and study instrumentation.

Purdue C Design Lab ↗ · advised by Prof. Karthik Ramani ↗
XR Authoring · Agents2025 — 2026Under review

In-situ authoring for embodied agents in AR

Desktop authoring gets awkward when an interaction depends on what someone is looking at, pointing to, or doing in the room.

I designed a dual-view workflow that lets creators define interactions in the physical scene while editing the larger task structure in a world-in-miniature. I also built the multimodal compiler/runtime that turns speech, gaze, pointing, and user pose into grounded references and executable agent behavior.

My role: interaction concept, task representation, multimodal compiler/runtime, Unity/Meta Quest implementation, and evaluation. A two-session study with 12 participants achieved a 79.8 ± 10.8 SUS score.

Purdue C Design Lab ↗ · advised by Prof. Karthik Ramani ↗
Egocentric AI · Memory2026 — Present

Long-horizon memory for wearable AI

Hours of first-person video are easy to collect and surprisingly hard to use later.

I work on event-centric memory, hierarchical retrieval, and tool-based inspection for finding the right event, time, object state, or interaction in long egocentric recordings. The goal is to recover useful evidence without repeatedly sending an entire hour- or day-scale stream back through the model.

My role: long-video reasoning, retrieval design, data curation, post-training support, benchmark design, and accuracy–latency analysis for intelligent eyewear.

Egocentric AI research · supervised by Dr. Jingkang Yang ↗
3D Generation · Evaluation2025 — Present

Contact-aware 3D piano hand motion generation

A hand pose can look plausible and still press the wrong key.

I reframed MIDI-conditioned hand-motion generation around time-indexed 3D key-contact targets and developed contact-aware flow-matching objectives. I also built a hard-passage evaluation for large jumps, dense chords, and fast transitions, where wrong fingers and timing errors are much easier to expose.

My role: problem formulation, target representation, learning objectives, event-local refinement, and task-specific evaluation.

Purdue ECE ↗ · advised by Prof. Yung-Hsiang Lu ↗
Generative AI · Edge AI2025 — Present

Hardware-aware evaluation of text-to-image models

A model can win on image quality and still be a bad choice once you run it on an edge device.

I built a benchmark for seven text-to-image models that puts semantic alignment and perceptual quality next to end-to-end latency and peak GPU memory. I deployed every model on NVIDIA Jetson Orin so the quality-versus-cost trade-off is measured on the same hardware.

My role: benchmark design, prompt taxonomy, automated evaluators, edge deployment, and systems profiling.

Purdue ECE ↗ · advised by Prof. Yung-Hsiang Lu ↗
Wearable AI · Voice AgentsSummer 2026

Voice-first video assistance for AR glasses

On glasses, a vague voice request can't always fall back to a search-results screen.

At Eyedaptic ↗, I built an assistant that handles search, clarification, selection, resume, and playback through voice and typed tools. I also designed a two-stage retrieval and reranking pipeline over candidate videos, metadata, channel context, and local behavioral memory.

My role: agent/tool architecture, retrieval and ranking, personalization memory, Android/backend integration, and scenario evaluation. Mean top-1 score improved from 0.182 to 0.604 across a 10-scenario test set.

Prototype work. I also build small smart-glasses agents to test ideas around visual grounding, cross-frame retrieval, and tool execution before deciding whether they are worth turning into a larger study.

About

More than research & coursework.

I'm probably more interested in products than in any one technology. A good product starts with the person using it, so I often try to build a rough model of that person first—what they know, what they notice, what they expect, what they'll ignore, and what will surprise them.

That usually tells me where the interface should be simple, where the system needs to explain itself, and what should stay invisible. I like taking ideas end to end because a lot of assumptions only become obvious once something has to work on a real device.

How I think

First principles. I like reducing a messy problem until I can see the few relationships that actually matter. Daoist philosophy probably has something to do with that.

Products & mental models. I tend to model the person before the product: what they believe is happening, what they will try next, and where they are likely to get confused.

AI-native building. I learned software engineering while coding agents were becoming useful, so tools like Codex and Claude feel normal to me—just another part of how I build.
